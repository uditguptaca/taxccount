## Done while fixing a finding, beyond the list

## What the rules could not express
1. A condition on the year-end answer (`{ q: 'fye', is: '06-30' }`): refused by `integrity.ts` (see S3).
2. "Every two years" as a frequency label (S18).
3. A quarterly cycle that starts on a future date, and "last business day of the month" (S4, S5): lists of dates are used and need extending before 2029.
4. A weekend rule or a moved date on a researched (JSON) item: the importer fixes `roll: false` and drops `moved` (S16).
5. A penalty page of its own on a hand-written rule: `CDef` in `dsl.ts` has no `penaltySource`, so `us_990` and `us_tx_pir` set it by spreading the result of `C(...)`.
6. A base rule in a type defined only by the research files (S15).

## Noticed and left
- The IRS page "Check if you need to file a tax return" shows $17,550 for a single person aged 65 or older for 2025; 2025's other figures suggest $17,750. Not asserted either way.
- Florida's "later of" rule: with the federal date for June year ends now in the 4th month, the real Florida date for a year ending 30 June 2027 may be 30 October 2027; 1 October is shown, marked "please confirm".
- New Jersey's new-hire law now also covers people a business contracts with (P.L. 2025, c. 361); the row speaks of employees only.
- The Georgia Department of Labor has one page that says 20 days for new hires where its others say 10 (seen in search results only, not opened).
